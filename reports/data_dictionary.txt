5) Are These Datasets Sufficient to Test the Defined Hypotheses?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Retention label feasibility (needed for all hypotheses)
- Feasible: Yes.
  - You can compute retention from orders.csv using order_date per customer.
  - Two common index choices:
    - First order date (recommended for behavioral retention).
    - Signup date (useful if signup precedes first order materially).

Important measurement limitation (right-censoring / follow-up window)
- The latest order_date is 2026-04-14.
- To compute 30-day retention without bias, restrict index dates to <= 2026-03-15.
- To compute 90-day retention without bias, restrict index dates to <= 2026-01-14.
- If you do not apply a cutoff, late-cohort customers will look artificially “not retained” simply because there is not enough observation time.

Hypothesis 1: Delivery Reliability Drives Retention
- Testable with current data: Yes.
- Available fields:
  - promised_time_min, actual_time_min, delay_minutes, delivery_status
  - customer_rating (as an outcome/mediator proxy)
- Suggested metrics:
  - % delayed deliveries per customer, median delay_minutes per customer, average actual_time_min

Hypothesis 2: Service Failures Reduce Retention
- Testable with current data: Partially.
- Available proxies:
  - delivery_status (Delayed may be an experience failure proxy)
  - order_accuracy (Partial/Wrong as a fulfillment error proxy)
  - customer_rating (low ratings as dissatisfaction proxy)
- Key gaps (not present in any file):
  - Cancellations/failed deliveries (explicit status or cancellation reason)
  - Refunds/credits, dispute amounts, chargebacks
  - Customer support interactions (tickets, chats, calls), resolution time
  - Item-level issues (missing item vs wrong item), substitution flags/quality (especially for Grocery)
  - Re-delivery / replacement orders linked to issue resolution
- Impact of gaps:
  - You can test whether “Delayed” and “Partial/Wrong” correlate with retention,
    but you cannot isolate the effect of broader service failures (refunds/support)
    or distinguish failure types that often drive churn.

Hypothesis 3: Early Engagement Predicts Longer-Term Retention
- Testable with current data: Yes.
- Available fields:
  - order_date by customer_id (for counts in first 7/14 days, time-to-2nd order, etc.)
  - vendor_category, vendor_id, delivery_type (for early diversity / service-mode behavior)
- Suggested metrics:
  - orders_in_first_7d / 14d, days_to_second_order, vendor_count_first_14d, category_mix_first_14d
